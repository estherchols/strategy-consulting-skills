# Market Entry

Answer two questions: **should we enter**, and **if so, how?** End with a clear go/no-go recommendation.

## Step 1: Clarify the objective
Why does the client want to enter? Growth, diversification, following customers, using spare capacity, or defending against a competitor? Get the success criteria in numbers (for example, "$100M revenue within 5 years at ≥15% operating margin, with payback under 4 years").

## Step 2: Assess the market (is it attractive?)
- **Size and growth:** current size, CAGR, maturity stage (use the market-sizing approach).
- **Profitability:** typical margins; industry structure via **Porter's Five Forces** (rivalry, new entrants, substitutes, buyer power, supplier power).
- **Customers:** segments, needs, buying criteria, willingness to pay, switching costs.
- **Trends and risks:** regulation, technology, macro, political/FX risk (use PESTEL for international entry).

## Step 3: Assess the competition
- Key players, market shares, and concentration.
- Their positioning, strengths, and likely response to a new entrant (retaliation risk).
- Barriers to entry: scale economies, brand, distribution access, regulation, capital requirements, network effects.
- Unserved or underserved segments (white space).

## Step 4: Assess the company (do we have a right to win?)
- Relevant capabilities and assets: brand, technology, distribution, cost position, customer relationships.
- Capability gaps that must be closed.
- Synergies with the core business, and the risk of distraction or cannibalization.
- Financial capacity and risk appetite.

## Step 5: Choose the entry mode

| Mode | Speed | Cost | Control | Risk | Best when |
|------|-------|------|---------|------|----------|
| **Build** (organic) | Slow | Medium | Full | Execution risk | Capabilities exist, no good targets |
| **Buy** (acquisition) | Fast | High | Full | Integration & overpayment risk | Speed matters, attractive target available |
| **Partner** (JV, alliance, licensing, distribution) | Medium | Low | Shared | Partner misalignment | Local knowledge or regulatory access needed |

## Step 6: Financials
Estimate a simple business case: revenue ramp (share × market), costs (capex, fixed, variable), break-even, payback period, and NPV/IRR if data allows. Show base, downside, and upside cases.

## Step 7: Recommend

```markdown
## Recommendation (answer first)
Go / No-go / Go with conditions, plus the entry mode. One sentence with the key number.

## Why: three supporting arguments
1. Market: …
2. Right to win: …
3. Economics: …

## Entry plan
Mode, target segment, phasing (pilot → scale), first 90 days.

## Key risks & mitigations
## Next steps / what we'd need to validate
```

## Quality bar
- The recommendation follows from the client's stated objective and criteria.
- An attractive market alone isn't enough. The company must also have a right to win.
- Name the kill criteria: what you'd need to see in a pilot to stop.
