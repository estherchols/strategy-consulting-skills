# Growth Strategy & M&A

## Part A: Growth strategy

### 1. Establish the baseline and the gap
Momentum case: where will the business be in 3–5 years if nothing changes? Gap = ambition − momentum. That gap is what the growth strategy must fill.

### 2. Generate options with the Ansoff matrix

|  | Existing products | New products |
|--|------------------|--------------|
| **Existing markets** | **Market penetration:** share gain, pricing, frequency, retention | **Product development:** adjacent products, upsell, bundles |
| **New markets** | **Market development:** new geographies, segments, channels | **Diversification:** highest risk; usually via M&A |

Also consider: organic vs. inorganic, partnerships, new business models (subscription, platform, services).

### 3. Balance across horizons
- **Horizon 1:** extend and defend the core (most of the near-term growth).
- **Horizon 2:** build emerging businesses (adjacencies).
- **Horizon 3:** create options on future businesses (small bets).

### 4. Prioritize
| Option | Revenue potential | Margin | Time to impact | Investment | Risk | Right to win | Score |
Recommend a portfolio that closes the gap, phased over time.

## Part B: M&A evaluation

### 1. Strategic rationale
Why buy rather than build or partner? Typical rationales: scale/cost synergies, new capabilities or technology, market access, product extension, talent, or vertical integration. A deal without a clear rationale is a red flag.

### 2. Target screening
Define criteria (size, geography, capabilities, growth, margin, culture, availability/price), long-list targets, and score against the criteria.

### 3. Commercial due diligence
- **Market:** size, growth, and the drivers of the target's market.
- **Competitive position:** share, differentiation, customer stickiness (concentration, churn, NPS).
- **Business plan:** are management's projections realistic versus market growth and history?
- **Risks:** customer concentration, key-person dependency, regulatory, technology obsolescence.

### 4. Synergies
| Type | Examples | Realism |
|------|---------|---------|
| **Cost** | Procurement scale, overlapping SG&A, facility consolidation | Higher confidence; typically realized within 1–3 years |
| **Revenue** | Cross-selling, new channels, pricing power | Lower confidence; often overestimated, so haircut them |
Subtract one-time integration costs and the risk of dis-synergies (customer loss, talent attrition).

### 5. Valuation sense check
- Standalone value (DCF or comparable multiples) + synergies NPV − integration costs = maximum justifiable price.
- Compare with the asking price. If the premium exceeds the value of realistic synergies, value transfers to the seller.

### 6. Integration
Key risks: culture clash, talent loss, systems integration, customer disruption. Recommend a 100-day plan and an integration management office (IMO).

## Output

```markdown
## Recommendation (answer first)
## Rationale & fit
## Analysis: growth options table, or target assessment + due diligence findings
## Financials: synergies, valuation range, max price
## Risks & mitigations
## Next steps
```

## Pitfalls
- Overpaying because of deal momentum. Set a walk-away price up front.
- Counting revenue synergies at full value.
- Ignoring the cost of the capital and management attention a deal consumes.
