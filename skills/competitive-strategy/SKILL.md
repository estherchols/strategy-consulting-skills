# Competitive Strategy

Diagnose where a company stands and recommend where to play and how to win. Pick the 2–3 frameworks that fit the question rather than running all of them.

## Framework selection

| Question | Use |
|---------|-----|
| Is this industry attractive? Why are margins high or low? | **Porter's Five Forces** |
| Where do we create value and cost? | **Value chain analysis** |
| Overall situation snapshot | **3Cs** (Company, Customers, Competitors) + **SWOT** |
| How should we compete? | **Generic strategies** + **Where to play / How to win** |
| Which businesses in a portfolio to invest in or exit? | **Growth-share matrix** / GE-McKinsey matrix |
| Can the organization execute the strategy? | **7S model** |
| How to respond to a disruptor or price war? | **Competitive response analysis** (below) |

## Frameworks

### Porter's Five Forces
Rate each force High/Medium/Low with evidence: rivalry among competitors, threat of new entrants, threat of substitutes, buyer power, and supplier power. Conclude with overall industry attractiveness, and which forces the company can shape.

### Value chain
Map primary activities (inbound logistics, operations, outbound logistics, marketing and sales, service) and support activities (infrastructure, HR, technology, procurement). For each, ask whether it is a source of cost advantage, differentiation, or disadvantage relative to competitors.

### Generic strategies
Cost leadership, differentiation, or focus (cost or differentiation in a niche). Warn against being "stuck in the middle" without a clear basis for advantage.

### Where to play / How to win
- **Where to play:** segments, geographies, channels, products, and value chain steps.
- **How to win:** the value proposition and the capabilities that make it hard to copy.
- Test with the question "what must be true?" for the strategy to succeed.

### Growth-share matrix (portfolio)
Plot business units by market growth (y) vs. relative market share (x): Stars (invest), Cash cows (harvest and fund others), Question marks (invest selectively or exit), Dogs (divest or fix). Note the limitations: share isn't always the driver of profitability.

### 7S model
Check alignment across Strategy, Structure, Systems, Shared values, Style, Staff, and Skills. Misalignments explain why strategies fail in execution.

### Competitive response (to a new entrant or disruptor)
1. Understand the threat: their economics, target segment, and likely trajectory.
2. Quantify exposure: which customers and revenue are at risk?
3. Options: ignore, match, differentiate, flank (fighting brand), partner/acquire, or change the game (new business model).
4. Weigh each option by cost, cannibalization, and the competitor's likely counter-move.

## Output

```markdown
## Recommendation (answer first)
## Situation: industry structure & our position (frameworks used, with evidence)
## Strategic options (2–4, with pros/cons)
| Option | Value created | Feasibility | Risk | Fit with capabilities |
## Recommended strategy: where to play, how to win, what must be true
## Implications: org, investment, and the first moves
```

## Quality bar
- Frameworks are tools, not deliverables. Every framework output should end in a "so what".
- Back each claim with evidence (data, examples), or flag it as an assumption.
- Strategy means choices. State explicitly what the company will **not** do.
